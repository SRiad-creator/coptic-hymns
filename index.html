Understanding Hazzat & Rites

What are Coptic Hazzat?

The Hazzat (musical shakes) represent the traditional, oral Coptic method of indexing melodies. Because Coptic hymns consist of long, complex pitch waves (where a single syllable of text might span several minutes of singing), the notation maps represent the rise, fall, flat hold, and oscillation of the voice.

How to read the Notation Map:

  • Flat Hold: Represents a straight, steady tone without throat vibrato.
  • Vocal Shake: Indicates a throat wave or pitch wobble on the syllable.
  • Pitch Rise: Sing the syllable transitioning to a higher pitch scale.
  • Pitch Fall: Sing the syllable gliding down to a lower pitch scale.

Structure of the Divine Liturgy

The Coptic Divine Liturgy follows a structured order of services:

  • Offering of the Lamb: Preparation of the bread and wine — includes the Hymn of Blessing (Ten Ouosht), Shere Maria, Procession (Alleluia Fai Pe Pi), and Doxa Patri.
  • Liturgy of the Word: Scripture readings and responses — includes the Trisagion, Pauline Epistle, Catholic Epistle, Acts, Psalm & Gospel, and Gospel Response (O-oniato).
  • Liturgy of the Faithful: The Eucharistic prayers — includes the Creed, Anaphora, Institution Narrative, and Communion hymns.

📱 Coptic Reader App

The Coptic Reader app (by the Diocese of the Southern US) is the gold standard for following along with any Coptic service. It automatically adjusts hymns, readings, and responses for the correct season and day. Available for iOS and Android.
